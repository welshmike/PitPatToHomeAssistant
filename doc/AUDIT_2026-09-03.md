# PaceKeeper code audit (pre M5Stack Dial migration)

Date: 2026-09-03. Scope: working tree at commit 24ca1bc plus ~630 lines of uncommitted changes.
Build check: `pio run -e usb` succeeds. Flash 82.9% of the 1.25 MB OTA slot, RAM 25.9%.

## What is in good shape

- Clear module split: `ba05protocol` (wire format), `TreadmillHandler` (BLE + session), `TreadmillState` (NVS totals + calibration), `MqttView` (HA discovery + publish), `main.cpp` (glue).
- Q1 BLE behaviour is well understood and documented in `doc/Q1_BLE_NOTES.md`. The kick-phase, supervision-timeout drop, zombie detection and GATT-cache reuse are all hard-won and should be preserved verbatim through the migration.
- All MQTT publishes already run on the main loop via the `m_newDataAvailable` flag. That is the right shape for adding a display.

## Findings, ordered by impact on the migration

### 1. BLE stops whenever WiFi or MQTT is down (correctness, belt safety)

`loop()` returns early when WiFi is disconnected or `connectToMqtt()` fails, and `treadmill.handle()` is only reached after both succeed. Keepalives must go out every 200 ms against a 6 s supervision timeout. A broker restart or a WiFi blip longer than 6 s mid-walk drops the BLE link, and the pad kills the motor when BLE drops (the "thrown off" case the project's own notes describe).

`setup()` also spins forever waiting for WiFi, so with no WiFi the device never reaches BLE at all, and the 300 s watchdog then reboots it in a loop. On the Dial the whole point of local controls is to work without Home Assistant, so this must change.

Fix: call `treadmill.handle()` unconditionally every loop; turn WiFi and MQTT into non-blocking state machines with their own backoff; drop the forced restart on WiFi loss.

### 2. `TreadmillHandler` mixes four concerns and repeats the delta maths four times

`TreadmillHandler.cpp` (852 lines) plus its header (440 lines, with large inline `onDisconnect`) hold: BLE link management, Q1 kick-phase heuristics, session accounting (connection baselines, pause snapshot, resume suppression), the idle/pause timers, and NVS settings. The "current minus connection base, clamp at zero, derive steps" block appears in `onDisconnect`, pause-stop, session-end and live-totals.

Why it matters now: the Dial needs the same session data for the screen, and a second command source. Extract:

- `SessionTracker` (or a `computeDelta()` helper) owning baselines, pause snapshot and the four delta calculations.
- Keep `TreadmillHandler` as the BLE link layer only.

### 3. Command handling is duplicated in `main.cpp` and will be duplicated again by the Dial

Each MQTT handler does: call the treadmill, build an optimistic `TreadMillData`, publish it. Encoder and button input will need the identical sequence. Speed constants are scattered: raw factor 1600 and cap 6080 in `main.cpp`, 3.8 mph in `mqttview.cpp`, `START_SPEED` 994 in the handler.

Fix: a `TreadmillController` intent layer with `start()`, `pause()`, `stop()`, `adjustSpeed(delta)`, `setSpeed(mph)` that clamps, applies optimistic state, and notifies all views (MQTT and display). Constants move to `platform.h`.

### 4. Shared state is not protected (latent, more likely with a display)

`notifyCallback` runs on the NimBLE task and writes the ~80-byte `m_lastData` struct field by field. `getLastData()` copies it from the main loop. The `volatile bool` flags are fine, the struct copy is not: a display refreshing at 10 to 30 Hz raises the odds of a torn read (for example speed from the new packet, status from the old).

Also `m_state.addSession()` performs an NVS write from inside the NimBLE callback (in `onDisconnect` and `notifyCallback`), blocking the BLE host task for milliseconds.

Fix: guard `m_lastData` with a FreeRTOS mutex or a sequence-counter double buffer; queue session commits to the main loop. The `m_publishingConfigs` guard in `MqttView` is now dead (all publishes are on one task) and its comment is misleading; remove it.

### 5. Main loop runs at 10 Hz

`delay(100)` at the end of `loop()`. Acceptable for MQTT, too slow for encoder and button feel, and adds up to 100 ms jitter to the 200 ms keepalive. Remove the delay; rate-limit the display redraw instead.

### 6. Toolchain is too old for the Dial

Installed: espressif32 platform 5.4.0 (Arduino-ESP32 2.0.x), which has no `m5stack-stamps3` board. Registry latest is 7.1.0; 6.13.0 is the last of the 6.x line. Two PlatformIO cores are installed (6.1.16 in `~/Library/Python/3.9` and 6.1.19), which PIO itself warns about. Libraries in use: NimBLE-Arduino 2.5.1 (supports S3), ArduinoJson 7.4.3, PubSubClient 2.8, mqttdisco (git). M5Dial library is 1.0.3 and depends on M5Unified and M5GFX.

### 7. Board-specific pins are hard-coded

`LED_BLE_PIN 2` in `platform.h`. On the Dial GPIO2 is PORT.B. Introduce a `board.h` selected by build flag so the DevKit build keeps working during the transition.

### 8. Uncommitted work

Nine files changed and uncommitted: idle-disconnect timer, pause timeout, pending-command queue, restore-calibration topic, step-length entity removed, platformio.ini restructured into `usb` and `ota` envs. `partitions.csv` is untracked but referenced by `platformio.ini`, so a fresh clone does not build. `Archive.zip` is untracked and should be deleted or ignored. Commit this before any migration work so there is a known-good DevKit baseline to bisect against.

### 9. Small items

- `handleSpeedCommand` calls `atof` on the raw MQTT payload, which PubSubClient does not NUL-terminate. Copy into a bounded buffer first. The `String((char*)payload).substring(0, length)` pattern elsewhere has the same over-read.
- `TreadMillData::fwVersion` is always 0 but still published.
- `VERSION "2026.1.1"` is static; consider deriving from git.
- Flash at 82.9%: adding M5GFX and fonts will exceed the 1.25 MB slot on the DevKit. The Dial has 8 MB, so use a 2 x 3 MB OTA partition table there.

## M5Stack Dial v1.1 hardware facts relevant to the design

- MCU: StampS3A, ESP32-S3FN8, dual core 240 MHz, 8 MB flash, no PSRAM. WiFi and BLE coexist on S3 the same way as on the classic ESP32.
- Display: 1.28 inch round 240x240, GC9A01 over SPI (RS G4, MOSI G5, SCK G6, CS G7, RST G8, backlight G9).
- Touch: FT3267 capacitive, I2C (SDA G11, SCL G12, INT G14).
- Encoder: quadrature on G41 (A) and G40 (B), 16 detents and 64 pulses per revolution. The encoder has no push switch.
- Buttons: WAKE (BtnA in M5Unified, GPIO42) and RST on the body. The screen is not a mechanical button.
- Buzzer on G3. RTC BM8563 and RFID WS1850S on the internal I2C bus.
- PORT.A (I2C) G13/G15, PORT.B (GPIO) G1/G2. HOLD pin GPIO46 must be driven high to stay on when battery powered.
- Power: USB-C 5 V, rear screw terminal 6 to 36 V, or 3.7 V battery connector.
- Library: `m5stack/M5Dial` 1.0.3 exposes `M5Dial.Display` (M5GFX), `Touch`, `BtnA`, `Encoder` (PJRC interrupt encoder, `read()` / `readAndReset()`), `Speaker`, `Rtc`, `Rfid`, `Power`. `begin(cfg, enableEncoder, enableRFID)`.
- v1.1 vs v1.0: StampS3A module with improved antenna, larger tactile buttons, lower power. Pinout unchanged.

1. Flash `devkit-usb`. HA shows the same PaceKeeper device and entities; retained totals unchanged.
2. Start / pause / resume / stop from HA. Session summary and totals match a manual delta.
3. Set speed from HA slider; stop via slider at 0.
4. Mid-walk WiFi outage 30 s: belt keeps running, HA catches up.
5. Boot with WiFi off: belt connects; HA appears when WiFi returns.
6. Idle disconnect and pause timeout still fire (set to 1 min each for the test).
7. Kick phase from cold connect settles in about 2 min as before.
8. With a walk running, stop the MQTT broker for 60 s then restart it — belt keeps running, HA recovers within one backoff period.
9. After WiFi returns from an outage, confirm `esp32-<chipid>.local` still resolves for OTA.

## Phase C hardware checklist (Dial)

Real-belt verification for the `dial-usb`/`dial-ota` build, to be run by Mike once Plan 2 is
implemented, before calling the Dial migration done.

1. Each screen appears in the right state: Disconnected (last-session summary), Connecting
   (attempt count), Starting (pulsing "STARTING" during belt countdown), Running, Paused
   (dimmed Running with pulsing label) — walk through all five and confirm each one only shows
   for its own state.
2. Tap the screen while Disconnected: the screen switches to Connecting (reachable by tapping
   start while the belt is unreachable — a queued start does not jump straight to Starting), the
   belt connects, and then starts (Connecting, then COUNTDOWN/Starting, then Running). Tap or
   hold on the Connecting screen cancels the connect instead of trying to start again.
3. Tap while Running: belt pauses, screen shows Paused.
4. Tap while Paused: belt resumes at the previously commanded speed, not the minimum.
5. Hold the screen for about 1 second while Running or Paused: belt stops.
6. Press the side WAKE button on any screen (Disconnected, Connecting, Starting, Running,
   Paused): belt stops (emergency stop, always — on Connecting/Disconnected this is a harmless
   refusal since there's no belt link yet, and it does not cancel an in-progress connect; only
   tap/hold on the Connecting screen do that).
7. Rotate the dial 5 clicks: exactly one speed command goes out, about 0.4 s after the last
   click, changing speed by 0.5 mph (5 x 0.1 mph); the centre overlay shows the target speed
   during the wait and reverts to elapsed time about 1.5 s after the last click.
8. Rotate the dial while stopped or disconnected: the belt starts and runs at the dialed speed
   (not the default start speed).
9. Idle power: leave the screen on Disconnected/Stopped for 2 minutes — brightness drops to
   20%; leave it for 10 minutes total — backlight goes off. The first tap/turn/hold after either
   only wakes the screen; the belt does not react to that first input.
10. Buzzer tones: one short click on an accepted tap, two short beeps on stop, and a low buzz
    when a command is refused (e.g. during the post-connect cooldown, or a disconnect attempt
    while the belt is still active).
11. Home Assistant still shows the same `PaceKeeper` device and entities, and retained totals
    are unchanged, after switching from the DevKit to the Dial.
12. `pio run -e dial-ota -t upload` succeeds against `pacekeeper-dial.local` after an initial
    USB flash.
13. Kick phase from a cold connect settles (no BLE drop/reconnect loop) in about 2 minutes, same
    as the DevKit.
14. Mid-walk: turn the WiFi AP off for 30 s, then stop the MQTT broker for 60 s (with the belt
    still running throughout) — the belt keeps running through both outages and HA catches up
    once WiFi/MQTT return.
15. The Connecting screen shows an incrementing attempt count while retrying (once there's been
    at least one attempt — otherwise just "Connecting..." with no count), and a tap or a long
    press during Connecting cancels the connect attempt instead of trying to start/stop a belt
    that isn't running yet.
16. The speed overlay (target speed + amber ring) and the hold-to-stop/cancel progress arc both
    appear on every screen, not just Running/Paused — e.g. rotating the dial while Disconnected
    or Connecting shows the overlay there too, and holding on the Connecting screen fills the red
    arc the same as holding on Running does.
